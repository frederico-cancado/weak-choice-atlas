Algebraic closures:
existence without uniqueness

Frederico Cançado15 October 2026Preliminary preprint

A symmetric model in pure set theory separates the existence of algebraic closures from their uniqueness—even with dependent choice. Two further results connect uniqueness to finite choice and compactness for finite group torsors.

Read the article ↗Source & research record

These are preliminary manuscript results. The arguments have not been externally peer reviewed or formally verified. The article makes no claim of historical novelty.

The two assertions

E
ExistenceEvery field has an algebraic closure.
U
Conditional uniquenessWhenever two algebraic closures of the same field are given, there is an isomorphism between them respecting the specified embeddings of the base field.

Uniqueness concerns the supplied closures and the specified base field. Its formulation does not itself assert that a closure exists.

Main results

A separation in pure ZF

Con(ZF) ⇒ Con(ZF + DC + E + ¬U + ¬ACfin + ¬BPI).

Gödel’s constructible universe gives Con(ZF) ⇒ Con(ZFC), so the construction can use a ZFC ground universe while requiring only Con(ZF).

Existence therefore does not imply uniqueness over ZF, even with dependent choice. The two nonisomorphic closures can be taken over a specified well-orderable field of characteristic zero.

Existence and uniqueness imply finite choice

BPI ⇒ (E ∧ U) ⇒ ACfin.

The implication from BPI is the established result of Banaschewski. The article gives a direct argument for the second implication: an isomorphism between closures of two generic algebraic extensions selects a member of every set in an arbitrary family of nonempty finite sets.

A finite-group correspondence

Assuming E:   U ⇔ P ⇔ T.

P says that every coordinate projection from the inverse limit of a directed system of finite groups with surjective bonding homomorphisms is onto. T says that every directed surjective inverse system of nonempty finite group torsors has a coherent point.

Over ZF, P and T are equivalent and imply U together with ACfin. The converse from U is proved with E as an additional hypothesis.

How the separation is built

  1. Construct closures with controlled symmetry. A free group acts on Cohen forcing. A semilinear lifting argument builds Boolean-valued algebraic closures while keeping each section orbit small enough to have an admissible support.
  2. Realize the closures inside the model. Equivariant names represent the closure and its base embedding as hereditarily symmetric sets. This establishes E for every field in the symmetric extension.
  3. Obstruct finite choice. A parity construction gives a well-ordered family of pairs with no selector. Together with (E ∧ U) ⇒ ACfin, this yields ¬U. The forcing and support filter also preserve DC.

The main article includes the complete construction and proofs. The supplement collects further choice consequences, restricted equivalences, and permutation models with atoms.

What remains undecided here

The reverse implication U ⇒ E over pure ZF is not settled by this construction. Nor is unrestricted finite-group torsor compactness identified with BPI. These are limits of the present results, not claims about the status of every result in the literature.
